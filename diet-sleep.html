Part 2

Sleep

Why sleep matters

What my research showed about sleep and training.

Muscle recovery

Much of the body's growth hormone is released during deep sleep, which supports repair after training.

What I noticed[Your observation]

Energy

Teenagers are recommended 8–10 hours a night. Falling short builds up as tiredness through the week.

What I noticed[Your observation]

Exercise performance

Poor sleep can reduce endurance, reaction time and the motivation to train.

What I noticed[Your observation]

Concentration

Sleep helps the brain store what was learned during the day, which matters for school as much as sport.

What I noticed[Your observation]

Mood

Short sleep makes stress and irritability more likely, and makes it harder to stick to good habits.

What I noticed[Your observation]

Consistency

Going to bed and waking at similar times, including weekends, keeps the body clock steady.

What helped my sleep

The changes that made the biggest difference.

  • Consistent bedtime

    Same bedtime on school nights, and no more than an hour later at weekends. [Your example]

  • Reducing screen time

    Phone off and charging outside the bedroom by 21:30. [Your example]

  • Planning schoolwork earlier

    Starting homework straight after school so it didn't push bedtime back. [Your example]

  • Avoiding very late workouts

    Finishing training at least 2–3 hours before bed so I could wind down. [Your example]

  • Creating a nighttime routine

    The same steps each night: shower, prepare bag, read for 15 minutes, lights out. [Your example]

  • Tracking sleeping hours

    Writing down bed and wake times every morning showed patterns I hadn't noticed. [Your example]
